# What is a decision tree?

Imagine playing **20 Questions**. One person thinks of something, and your job is to figure out what it is by asking only yes/no questions. If you ask random questions, you’ll waste turns quickly. But if you ask smart questions that split the possibilities—like “Is it alive?” or “Is it bigger than a car?”—you narrow down the options much faster. Each answer pushes you down a different path of reasoning until eventually you’re confident enough to guess the object.

A **decision tree** in machine learning works in almost exactly the same way. The model learns a sequence of yes/no questions that progressively narrow down the possibilities. Each question splits the data into groups, just like how your questions in 20 Questions split the set of possible objects. As you move down the tree, the possibilities get more specific until you reach a final answer or prediction. In other words, a decision tree is basically the computer learning how to play a really efficient game of 20 Questions.
